07

The showroom stays inside your session

Marble Studio is a lightweight rendering scene opened in a full-screen modal. Your match pauses underneath. Closing it restores the same document, active level, shot count, and menu state. No deep-link navigation or restart is required. It has five camera poses, automatic showcase, spin, touch orbit, filtering, and photo downloads. Match-preservation check.

07.A

A new cast, not a repaint

The rejected superhero-style meshes have been replaced by three Microsoft Rocketbox avatars under the MIT license. They have realistic proportions, actual clothing, hair, and detailed skin/fabric textures, with native idle, walk, and conversation animation. Large source TGA maps were converted to compact 1024px WebP textures. This is a more realistic game-character set, not film-quality digital humans or facial performance capture.

07.B

Arabic blog and a lighter phone profile

The blog now supports Arabic/English, RTL, translated technical content, captions, and feedback controls. Switching languages preserves typed notes. Touch devices use 1024px terrain textures, 1x pixel ratio, smaller shadows/reflections, and no depth of field by default. Automatic character introductions are off on mobile and can be enabled in settings.

The production game, studio modal, and Arabic dev log passed checks under a GitHub Pages-style repository path. Touch orbit and layout were tested in a phone-sized desktop-browser emulation, not a physical-phone performance certification. Publishing is prepared but still awaits the intended personal GitHub sign-in; the work account has not been used. Mobile and project-path report.

05.A

One hundred actual material variants

Ribbon glass, cat's-eye glass, cloudy glass, trapped bubbles, opaque candy swirls, porcelain, polished metal, pearl, agate, and aventurine. Each family has ten palette/pattern combinations. The collection has name search and material filters. These are 100 variants across ten material models, not 100 separate physical substances; classic match weights stay equal.

05.B

Six playable experiments, 240 generated courses

Open Play Lab to choose Marble Odyssey, Pulse Arena, Sky Ribbons, Moon Marble, Last Light, or The Lost Tin. Each family has forty seeded courses across five difficulty tiers. Terrain shape, gates, barriers, wind, and surface layout vary with the seed and tier. These are procedural course variants, not 240 hand-authored chapters or 240 independent mechanics.

WASD or arrows roll the marble; Space jumps, E pulses, and F toggles glide. On-screen controls also support touch. A speech-bubble button opens a verdict and note field at any time. Records and feedback save separately from the campaign. The six modes have been checked for terrain contact, movement, powers, filtering, and returning to the story. All 240 configurations are generated deterministically, but have not each been playtested end to end.

Experimental-mode browser report. The bank-shot aiming assist in the classic campaign remains experimental: rail collisions are fixed, but its intended-shot win test still fails. You chose to defer that tuning and focus this build on visuals and mode review. The failure remains visible in the tests.

05.C

Someone to meet. A reason to return.

You are Nour. An old marble tin recalls the friends who filled it. Before Mariam leaves for college, you travel to bring the old circle back together for one last neighborhood final. Each destination introduces a friend, an exchange, and a challenge.

The free Quaternius CC0 character models use compatible walk, talking, interaction, and kneeling clips. Clothing is a shader treatment on the base models. Dialogue is subtitled; optional narration uses your browser's available speech voices, not studio voice acting or lip synchronization. Character and animation provenance.

The language selector updates Arabic/English without resetting the match. Debug unlock opens all twelve campaign events without awarding medals or overwriting earned progress. Presentation and save-isolation report.

05.D

Places worth looking up for

The locations are authored, recognizable approximations, not geographic reconstructions or scanned monuments. Al-Muizz and Khan el-Khalili still reuse some neighborhood architecture; their next art pass needs more location-specific facades and street dressing. The Sphinx is a distant simplified silhouette. Poly Haven supplies the scanned ground, plaster, props, and HDR environment.

ARCHIVE 03

Where the game started

Pull the blue marble back and release, or set aim and power and press Flick. Knock targets fully beyond the chalk circle. Solo practice has no shot limit; Seven shots ends after seven flicks. New round restores the board.

Cannon-es handles rigid-body collisions at a fixed 240 Hz simulation step. The camera remains usable while marbles roll. Contact sounds are synthesized, not field recordings.

Drag empty ground to orbit; use the wheel or pinch to zoom. Camera buttons provide street, aim, overhead, and macro views. Space flicks, R resets, and H hides the interface. Escape or a double-click restores it. Settings contain match mode, resolution, sound, depth of field, and exposure.

Mouse and real emulated touch drags both scored and returned to a settled next turn. The mobile image is a 390 x 844 desktop-GPU emulation; physical-phone performance remains untested.

05

What is real, and what is approximated

Actual ray tracing, without a progressive path tracer

The marble shader analytically intersects spheres and the ground, splits reflection and refraction paths, and uses Fresnel weighting and distance-based absorption. Coloured interiors use fixed-step volume sampling. The shader has a bounded ray budget and no per-frame random sampling or temporal accumulation.

This is GPU shader ray tracing through WebGL 2, not direct access to RTX hardware ray-tracing cores, and not full-scene Monte Carlo path tracing.

The street, lighting, and caustic tradeoffs

The street is rasterized with Three.js PBR materials, a Poly Haven HDR environment, shadow maps, and deterministic spatial depth of field. A local cubemap captures the static street for reflected and transmitted surroundings. It updates when the lighting preset changes; distant reflected architecture is not ray-intersected triangle by triangle.

The small pools of concentrated light beneath the glass are analytic approximations, not photon-traced caustics. Diffuse bounce lighting is approximated by the HDR environment and fill lights. The soil scan has surface normals, but its physical collision surface is flat; the displacement map is downloaded for a later terrain pass and is not applied in this checkpoint.

What is not finished

This is an expanded browser prototype, not a finished AAA production. It now has campaign progression, simple rivals, a stylized animated cast, bilingual introductions, and experimental terrain modes. It does not yet have a high-fidelity hand-flick animation, studio voice acting, facial performance capture, multiplayer, a large bespoke story campaign, or recorded Egyptian street ambience. The bank-shot assist needs tuning. Course completion and balance need broader playtesting, especially across the 240 generated variants.

World reflections use a cubemap, caustics are approximated, and macro reflections can expose resolution and aliasing limits. Experimental terrain has matching heightfield collision and shader height sampling; classic boards remain mostly flat. Physical mobile hardware has not been benchmarked. The rules are proposed adaptations for your review, not a universal claim about Egyptian street rules.
